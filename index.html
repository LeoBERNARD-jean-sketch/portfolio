R&D – Final-Year (5th Year) Materials Engineering Internship – 26 weeks

ALTEN SE, Toulouse (France) · 09/2025 – 03/2026

Carbon Fiber & Thermoplastic Composites: Optimization, Processing & Feasibility

Context

Final-year internship in ALTEN’s Innovation Department, within the Zéphir project aiming to break windsurfing speed records, with industrial and academic partners (ICA laboratory, Arkema, Alpha Recyclage Composite, and 27-time windsurfing world champion Antoine Albeau, etc.). In 09/2025 the manufacturing process was chosen, but not the materials, combinations and parameters. Earlier recycling routes degraded the fibres too much (especially their shape) for high-stress use. A fairly new vapo-thermolysis process, developed by Alpha Recyclage Composite, recovers both matrix and fibre in a better state, as a usable non-woven veil. Only one application was known so far: the European CIDER project, which remained a prototype. The windsurf board is meant, above all, to be a first direct and concrete application.

Objectives

  • Contribute to the making of an eco-engineered windsurf board by working on its materials, combinations and parameters, integrating recycled and recyclable materials (including recycled carbon fibre) while combining high performance, industrial feasibility and reduced environmental impact. A bonus goal: present the prototype at JEC 2026 (Arkema stand).
  • Demonstrate the feasibility of recycled carbon fibre in a high-performance (non-aerospace) application, and the benefit of a route that recovers both fibre and matrix. Building the prototype is also a way to demonstrate the soundness and value of the recycling project itself.

Work

  • Experimental: processing and characterization of recycled carbon fibre reinforcements; drapability studies on 3D-printed prototypes of the board shape; infusion trials; process and design optimization (plies, fibre type, fibre volume fraction…) with a Taguchi design of experiments; tensile and dissolution tests.
  • Numerical: image-based analysis of fibre orientation distributions to estimate effective mechanical properties; video analysis of infusion to extract permeability; process simulations to optimize infusion and reduce defects such as dry spots.
  • Environmental: Life Cycle Assessment comparing design scenarios to support material and process selection, and to verify that the choices made remain consistent with the goal of a board with a lower environmental impact.
  • Communication: comprehensive state-of-the-art report, technical reports, weekly project presentations, coordination with external consultants.

Results

This work contributed to the understanding and initial qualification of recycled carbon fibre reinforcements for structural applications, demonstrating their relevance for high-performance composite systems and supporting the development of more sustainable and efficient composite structures. It also demonstrated the consistency and feasibility of the project with respect to its specifications. The prototype was built and exhibited at the Arkema stand at JEC, Paris, March 2026.

Evaluation

Grade A (the highest grade) awarded for this internship, based on a written report and an oral defence assessed by the university, and on the evaluation of my company supervisor.

R&D – 4th-Year Materials Engineering Internship – 26 weeks

OPmobility (H2-POWER division), Genk (Belgium) · 02/2024 – 08/2024

High-performance polymers for composite structures (thermoset resins, carbon fiber composites)

Context

Internship in OPmobility’s R&D center, in an applied research environment on high-pressure hydrogen vessels: carbon fibre composite structures and thermoset resin systems, with a focus on resin behaviour and process optimisation. At the start, the resin’s precise behaviour and a characterization protocol were not established, no predictive model existed, and in-house knowledge was limited.

Objectives

  • Characterize a resin to build its TTT diagram and model its curing with kinetic models (e.g. Kamal–Sourour) to understand and predict its behaviour.
  • Link glass transition temperature (Tg), degree of cure, electrical properties and viscosity.
  • Validate with thermocouples embedded in real vessels, and establish a protocol to characterize future resins more efficiently.

Work

  • Experimental: more than 300 tests on fast-curing thermoset resins used in wet filament winding (iterative, trial-and-error approach): DSC for curing kinetics and TTT diagrams; cure-simulator experiments on cure/viscosity/electrical/Tg relationships; analysis of thermocouple data from real manufacturing conditions.
  • Modelling: calibration of curing kinetic models under different thermal cycles; Python automation of data processing; collaboration with materials and simulation teams.
  • Support: quality control (density measurements to identify other issues; DSC to verify that the resin was properly cured) and a cost model (cost per vessel vs. number of vessels).

Results

This work contributed to a better understanding of resin curing kinetics and to the development of predictive models for composite processing. It also enabled the identification of the key experimental tests required to efficiently characterize new resin systems, allowing a more targeted and time-efficient experimental strategy for future studies. The final outcome was a contribution to process optimization, particularly enabling a significant reduction in the curing time (up to 70 % in some cases) of high-pressure hydrogen vessels.

Evaluation

Grade A (the highest grade) awarded for this internship, based on a written report and an oral defence assessed by the university, and on the evaluation of my company supervisor.
